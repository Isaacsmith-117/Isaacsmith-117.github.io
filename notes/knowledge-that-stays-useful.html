Knowledge that stays useful

A selected learning-library note · Curated with AI assistance · Reviewed 2026-09-13

Give each page a job

A useful article answers a recognizable question. Its title should help you find that answer later.

A practical structure:

  • Answer: explain the idea in a few sentences.
  • Example: show a concrete use or failure.
  • Source: link to the evidence behind factual claims.
  • Boundary: say when the advice does not apply.
  • Connection: link to a related concept or decision.

This is our editorial convention. It is inspired by a maintained, linked wiki.

Update with care

New evidence may narrow or contradict an old conclusion. Keep that disagreement visible until it is resolved. A new summary of an old summary is not independent confirmation.

For changing information, an update date means “checked on this date.” It does not promise that the claim remains current forever.

Example

A page called “Git without losing work” explains a specific distinction and gives a usable command. A page called “Miscellaneous development stuff” is much harder to find and maintain.
